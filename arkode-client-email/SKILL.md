# Arkode Client Email

## Overview

This skill helps write professional, consistent emails to Arkode clients across all communication scenarios.

**Keywords**: client email, client communication, professional email, business email, client outreach

---

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill when composing any email to Arkode clients, including:
- Initial client outreach and introductions
- Project status updates
- Meeting requests and scheduling
- Proposal and quote delivery
- Invoice and payment communications
- Project kickoff and onboarding
- General client correspondence

---

## Arkode Email Standards

### Brand Voice
- **Professional yet approachable** - Balance expertise with friendliness
- **Clear and concise** - Respect client's time
- **Solution-focused** - Emphasize outcomes and value
- **Proactive** - Anticipate needs and offer next steps

### Email Structure

**Subject Line:**
- Clear and specific
- Include project name when relevant
- Format: `[Project Name] - [Topic]` or `Arkode: [Topic]`

**Greeting:**
- Use client's preferred name
- Examples: "Hi [Name]," or "Hello [Name],"

**Body:**
1. **Opening** - Brief context or reference to previous conversation
2. **Main Content** - Key information, updates, or requests
3. **Action Items** - Clear next steps (if any)
4. **Closing** - Professional sign-off

**Signature:**
```
Best regards,
[Your Name]
Arkode
[Your Title]
[Email]
[Phone - if applicable]
```

## Email Templates by Type

### 1. Project Status Update

**Structure:**
- Summary of progress since last update
- Completed milestones
- Current focus areas
- Upcoming deliverables
- Any blockers or questions
- Next steps

**Tone:** Professional, transparent, optimistic

### 2. Meeting Request

**Structure:**
- Purpose of meeting
- Proposed agenda items
- Suggested time/date options
- Expected duration
- Meeting format (in-person, video, phone)

**Tone:** Respectful of their time, flexible, organized

### 3. Proposal/Quote Delivery

**Structure:**
- Thank them for the opportunity
- Brief recap of their needs
- Overview of proposed solution
- Next steps (review, questions, approval)
- Availability for discussion

**Tone:** Confident, value-focused, helpful

### 4. Invoice Communication

**Structure:**
- Reference to completed work
- Invoice details (amount, payment terms)
- Payment instructions
- Appreciation for their business
- Contact for questions

**Tone:** Professional, clear, appreciative

### 5. Project Kickoff

**Structure:**
- Excitement about the partnership
- Confirmation of project scope
- Team introductions
- Timeline overview
- Communication plan
- Next immediate steps

**Tone:** Enthusiastic, organized, collaborative

## Quality Checklist

Before sending, verify:
- [ ] Subject line is clear and specific
- [ ] Greeting uses client's preferred name
- [ ] Main message is concise (under 200 words if possible)
- [ ] Action items are clearly stated
- [ ] Tone matches the relationship and context
- [ ] No jargon without explanation
- [ ] Proper signature included
- [ ] Proofread for spelling/grammar

## Guidelines

### Information to Gather

Before composing, ask the user for:
- **Recipient name and context** (relationship, project)
- **Email purpose** (update, request, proposal, etc.)
- **Key information** to include
- **Urgency level** (affects tone and call-to-action)
- **Any specific asks** or action items

### Tone Adjustments

**First-time contacts:** More formal, establish credibility
**Ongoing projects:** Warm, collaborative, transparent
**Senior stakeholders:** Concise, high-level, strategic
**Technical contacts:** Can include more detail, technical terms
**Urgent matters:** Direct, clear action items, specific timelines

### What to Avoid

- Technical jargon without context
- Overly long paragraphs (>4 lines)
- Vague next steps
- Passive voice when active is clearer
- Excessive apologies or hedging
- Missing context or assumptions of knowledge

---

## Notes

- Always ask about client relationship context if unclear
- Adapt formality based on existing communication style
- When in doubt, err on the side of professionalism
- Include relevant project names for easy email threading
- Consider time zones when suggesting meeting times
